# Proposal — Studies and Free Game: two ways to play

**Status:** Proposal for director review. It changes no canonical document. It
is recorded under the specification change protocol in `AGENTS.md`: problem,
proposed change, affected specifications, compatibility impact, alternatives.
**Date:** 2026-09-26.
**Reviewed build:** `main` @ `171452d`. The deployed GitHub Pages build serves
the same asset hashes.
**Method:** one full session and part of a second, played in headless
Chromium; then the content pack analysed by script. §1.5 lists what this
method cannot judge.

---

## Summary

- The slice is a beautiful and honest **reader**. It is not yet a **game**: it
  poses no problem, so nothing can be solved and nothing can be got better at.
- The cause is a deliberate choice, not missing polish. The Schell review
  rejected the Challenge, Skill and Competition lenses "wholesale" to stop the
  old hidden-answer lottery. That removed the lottery and the problem together.
- **Proposal: two modes, one loop.** *Studies* frame the same verbs as authored
  problems with explicit, rule-checked goals. *Free Game* stays as it is and
  grows into an instrument. The first Studies are also the onboarding.
- **Two rules keep Studies honest:** every Study is solvable from public
  information (facets, faculties, cards), never from the answer key; and
  Studies give marks of form, never marks of truth.
- **The first step needs no schema change:** make the facets visible, author
  about twelve Studies in three families, and restart a Study instead of
  retracting a thread.
- Five decisions belong to the director (§8).

### 1.3 What works — keep it

- **The content.** The Bartók card — Lendvai's claim, Howat's 1983 critique,
  "treat the attribution as disputed" — is the strongest moment in the build.
  The evidence line above each card is the project's signature.
- **The Open Thread question** is specific and worth pursuing: "Is Invariance
  preserved exactly in each, or exactly in one and approximately in the other?"
- **The manuscript and armillary look**, the thread register, and *Copy the
  reading*.

### 1.4 What did not feel like play

1. **No question is posed.** Every pair is allowed and no pair is better. The
   threshold says so: "nothing you do is scored"; "You are not here to find
   answers."
2. **Nothing to get better at.** The outcome depends only on the pair. The
   intention changes one stance line; the card body stays the same
   (`findRelation(a, b)` takes no intention). Game ten is as hard as game one.
3. **No cost, so no decisions.** Threads are free and unlimited. Nine threads
   touched all twelve beads.
4. **The reward is reading.** Each documented card carries an insight of about
   sixty words, a counterpoint of about thirty, and its sources (means over all
   44 relations). The loop becomes: click, read, click, read.
5. **Motifs happen to the player.** The first Bridge formed at thread 3 without
   intent, and three Bridges formed in nine threads. Specification §2 plans the
   first motif for minutes 8–12. A reward the player did not aim for is a
   surprise, not an achievement.
6. **Most pairs are silent.** Of 276 possible pairs, 173 (63%) share no facet
   and have no relation, so they stay unlit; 59 (21%) become Open Threads; 44
   (16%) are documented. Attending Fibonacci Sequence gave five medium and six
   weak candidates, and no high one.
7. **The Lens has no verb.** It says "Disagreeing with a bead's place is the
   point", and offers no way to disagree.

### 1.5 Limits of this playtest

- **No audio.** Headless and muted. Sound carries much of the design, and
  nothing here judges it.
- Software rendering at 1280×800: no judgement of motion, comfort or camera.
- The player had read the documents first. This was not a naive first session.
- One full session and part of a second.

## 2. Diagnosis

**Self-determination theory** (Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski, 2006) says play holds
people when it meets three needs:

| Need | Now | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomy | very high | any pair, any intention, no goal |
| Competence | near zero | no goal to meet, no skill to grow |
| Relatedness | low | *Copy the reading* exists; no one to play with or for |

**MDA's eight kinds of fun** (Hunicke, LeBlanc & Zubek, 2004): sensation,
fantasy and submission are present. Discovery and expression are partial: the
player discovers by reading, and expresses with a pair and a verb. Challenge,
narrative and fellowship are absent.

**Koster** (*A Theory of Fun for Game Design*, 2004): fun is the feeling of
mastering a pattern. This game owns a real pattern language — 22 facets over
24 concepts — and never asks the player to learn it. The facets sit inside the
details card.

**Hesse.** The novel's Game is not only contemplation. It has a formal language
that players spend years learning, schools and exercises, and a ladder of
mastery that ends in the Magister Ludi. The slice builds the contemplation and
leaves out the discipline. Studies restore that half.

## 3. Proposal — two modes, one loop

Same verbs (Attend, arm an intention, weave), same content pack, same outcome
rules. Only the frame changes.

| | **Studies** (challenge) | **Free Game** (expression) |
|---|---|---|
| The Game as | a problem | an instrument |
| Starts from | an authored brief and a fixed set of beads | a seeded draw, as now |
| Goal | explicit and rule-checked | none; an optional subject |
| Constraint | facet rules; a soft thread budget | none |
| End | *solved* or *not yet*; marks of form; the Magister's solution | performance and portrait, as now |
| Iterate by | restarting the Study; later, retracting | retracting (later) |
| Teaches | the notation, the motifs, evidence literacy | — it spends what Studies taught |

**Why both.** Games that pair a constrained mode with a free one — Minecraft
(Survival and Creative), Kerbal Space Program (Career and Sandbox), Mini Metro
(Normal and Creative) — use the constrained mode to teach and motivate, and the
free mode to spend the mastery. A free mode alone risks blank-canvas paralysis.
A pure toy can hold players without goals (Townscaper) when every click pays
back in under a second. Here each action pays back in a paragraph.

### 3.1 The honesty rules for Studies

1. **Public information only.** A Study is solvable from what the player can
   see: facets, faculties, bead cards, the four intentions. No Study requires
   knowing which pairs are documented. Documented relations stay revelations,
   never requirements. This keeps product law 1: no hidden-answer hunt.
2. **Marks of form, never marks of truth.** A Study may characterise economy,
   range or variety. It never rewards a documented outcome over an Open Thread
   or an unlit one. This keeps CAV-006.
3. **No timer, unlimited attempts, nothing lost.** This keeps product law 3.

## 4. Components

### 4.1 Notation — make the facets visible (prerequisite)

- Each bead shows its two to four facets as small glyphs: 22 glyphs in all.
- While a thread is drawn, the facets both beads share light along it. The
  committed thread *carries* them.
- **Why:** the vocabulary becomes a learnable notation, like piece moves in
  chess or intervals in music. The player can see their own mastery grow.
- **Honesty:** facets are authored and validated, and already shown in the
  details card. Showing them exposes no endpoint list: only 44 of the 103
  facet-sharing pairs are documented.
- **Hooks:** the `conceptFacets` lookup; `sharedFacetsOf` in
  `src/domain/outcomes/resolveThreadOutcome.ts`.

### 4.2 Studies — five families

Every example below was checked by script against the current pack.

1. **Passage** — a ladder, after Lewis Carroll's *Doublets* (1879). Join bead A
   to bead B; every thread must share a facet.
   *Example:* The Möbius Band to Polyrhythm in three threads. Exactly four
   solutions exist, for example The Möbius Band —Self-Reference→ Cantor's
   Diagonal Argument —Discreteness→ Prime Numbers —No Common Measure→
   Polyrhythm.
   *Difficulty levers:* length; the number of solutions (Just Intonation to
   Polyrhythm has eighteen three-thread solutions, so it is easier); "a new
   facet at every step"; "cross a faculty at every step".
2. **Canon** — carry one facet through several beads and faculties.
   *Example:* Decomposition through all four faculties in three threads, for
   example Divisionism → The Overtone Series → The Fourier Series → Entropy.
   Every thread of that line is also documented, so the solver meets three
   revelations — but the Study does not require them. Only Discreteness and
   Decomposition reach all four faculties. Uses the existing Canon detector.
3. **Bridge** — make a named bead the only crossing between two faculty
   regions. Uses the existing Bridge detector.
4. **Dialectic** — weave a given Tension, then close a triangle with a third
   bead that grounds one pole. *Example:* Just Intonation against Equal
   Temperament; then The Overtone Series —Ground→ Just Intonation, and The
   Overtone Series to Equal Temperament. Both new threads share the facet
   Proportion. Uses the existing Dialectic detector, which needs the closed
   triangle, with one added rule: the grounding thread must share a facet, so a
   bare declaration does not count.
5. **Silence** — some briefs cannot be done. The right move is to declare
   "cannot be done" and name what is missing. *Example:* "Carry Proportion into
   Matter" — no Matter bead carries Proportion. This turns product law 8
   (silence over fabricated significance) into a move the player makes.

**Sample briefs, in the Game's voice:**

> **Waldzell · Study 3 — A Passage.** From *The Möbius Band* to *Polyrhythm* in
> three threads. Each thread carries a facet both of its beads hold.

> **Waldzell · Study 5 — A Canon.** Carry *Decomposition* through all four
> faculties. Three threads are enough.

> **Mariafels · Study 2 — A Silence.** Carry *Proportion* into Matter — or show
> that it cannot be carried.

**The solution sounds like its logic.** A solved Passage plays as one phrase,
bead motif to bead motif. A solved Canon plays as a canon, each motif entering
in imitation. A solved Dialectic plays the Tension, then its ground as a
cadence. This ties the new mechanics to the audio grammar, the project's
strongest asset. It is a direction for audio design, not a specification.

**The result, without a total:** *Solved* or *Not yet*. Then the Magister's
solution beside the player's, and marks of form such as *Economical* (the
fewest possible threads), *Wide* (four faculties) or *Varied* (a new facet at
each step). Hints are optional and free: one shows the next bead of the
Magister's line.

### 4.3 Retract — unweave

- `thread.retracted` is appended, never erased. The register shows the thread
  struck through and still readable: the manuscript behaviour the art direction
  is built on. First proposed as finding 6 of the Schell review.
- Studies need it to iterate. Free Game gains a way to stop standing behind a
  reading.
- **Cost:** a new event type, and ADR-013 froze schema version 1 for the slice.
  This is the largest compatibility cost in this proposal, so the first step
  avoids it: a Study restarts instead.

### 4.4 Thread budget — Studies only, soft

A brief may say "in four threads". Going over is allowed; the result says
"solved in six; the brief asked for four". Scarcity creates decisions, and a
soft budget avoids punishment.

### 4.5 A spine of mastery — Knecht's path

Group Studies into chapters named after places and ranks in Joseph Knecht's
life: Eschholz, Waldzell, Vicus Lusorum, Mariafels, Magister Ludi. Each chapter
ends in an examination: one Study with several goals. Passing earns the
chapter's title. Titles come from passing, never from volume or time played.

| Chapter | Teaches | Families |
|---|---|---|
| Eschholz | the glyphs; a thread carries a facet | Passage (two steps), Silence (easy) |
| Waldzell | motifs as goals | Passage (three steps), Canon, Bridge |
| Vicus Lusorum | several constraints at once | mixed, tight budgets |
| Mariafels | opposition and ground; the evidence line | Dialectic, Silence |
| Magister Ludi | composing to a brief | one examination |

The first two Eschholz Studies are the onboarding. They answer finding 1 of the
Schell review — "the first thirty seconds teach nothing" — in the world's own
terms.

### 4.6 Free Game upgrades

1. **A subject.** Optionally start with a theme: a facet, or an Open Thread
   from the shelf. The conclusion reads the web against it. This gives
   direction without a score. `TESTER-BRIEF.md` lists "a Game having a
   subject" as absent.
2. **The web as an instrument.** In Attunement, touch or drag across threads to
   sound them: play the web you built. Specification §13 already lets the
   player "conduct pulses, foreground a motif". In this playtest Attunement
   offered no visible verb.
3. **Cards in two layers.** Title, evidence line and one sentence first; the
   insight, counterpoint and sources on request. The loop keeps moving and the
   reading stays one step away.
4. **Motifs as intentions.** When a motif first forms, name the three forms in
   the margin as things one can compose: "a Canon carries one facet through
   three beads". Accidents become goals, with no counter.

### 4.7 Later — bigger bets

- **Daily Study.** One generated Study per date seed. Passage and Canon briefs
  can be solved by breadth-first search, so a generator can prove each daily
  brief solvable and grade it by length and number of solutions. A solution is
  shared as a link, because the event log replays deterministically. No
  backend, no leaderboard.
- **Open Thread quests.** A standing question on the shelf becomes a brief in
  a later Game: build a case for it, for example by grounding both ends through
  the shared facet. The Game marks it "argued in Game 7", never "true". The
  Master Plan's full version already names "persistent Open Threads that may be
  resolved later".
- **Ear Studies.** Hear two motifs transformed by one intention's grammar and
  name the intention. This doubles as the M4 blind pass, which
  `DIRECTOR-SIGNOFF.md` says cannot be self-administered.
- **Disputation.** A rule-driven opponent answers each thread with a Tension,
  or with the relation's own counterpoint (all 44 relations carry one); the
  player answers by grounding. Or two players, hot-seat, on one web. It has the
  highest ceiling for fun, and it conflicts with the slice's non-goal
  "multiplayer". Park it.
- **Claims and conjectures.** Mark a thread *Claim* or *Conjecture*; a Claim
  the record runs across is struck through. It rewards calibration. It edges
  toward predicting the record (product law 1), so test it on paper first.

## 5. The options, weighed

Fun impact is a design judgement, not a measurement. Effort is a rough size:
S ≈ one reviewable task; M ≈ two to four; L ≈ a milestone.

| Component | Adds | Fun impact | Effort | Touches | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Facet notation | literacy, mastery | high | M | — | build first |
| Studies: Passage, Canon, Silence | challenge, the "aha" | high | M | law 7, ADR-010 (Studies only) | build first |
| Soft budget, restart | decisions, iteration | medium | S | law 7 | with the Studies |
| Studies: Dialectic, Bridge | challenge | medium | S (detectors exist) | as above | second |
| Retract | iteration, ownership | medium | M | ADR-013 (schema v2) | after the spike |
| Cards in two layers | pacing | medium | S | — | any time |
| Web as instrument | sensation, expression | medium–high | M | — | Free Game track |
| Subject | direction | medium | S–M | — | Free Game track |
| Knecht's path titles | a long-term goal | medium | S | law 7 | after 12+ Studies |
| Daily Study and share link | ritual, relatedness | high | M | — (no backend) | after the spike |
| Ear Studies | challenge; M4 evidence | medium | S–M | — | cheap win |
| Open Thread quests | long-term goals | medium | L | persistence | later |
| Disputation | challenge, fellowship | high | L | slice non-goals | park |
| Claims and conjectures | risk, calibration | uncertain | M | law 1 | paper test first |

### 5.1 Recommended — option A, the Studies spike

Facet notation, `evaluateStudy`, twelve authored Studies in three families
(Passage, Canon, Silence), soft budgets and restart. No schema change. Then
judge by play whether Studies are fun before building retract, titles or the
Daily Study.

### 5.2 Alternatives considered

- **B — Free Game upgrades only** (subject, instrument, cards in two layers).
  Cheaper and safe, but it adds no competence, so it would not answer "it does
  not feel like a game".
- **C — Disputation first.** The highest ceiling, the highest risk and effort,
  and it crosses the slice's non-goals. Park it.
- **Do nothing.** Keep the build as a contemplative instrument for one
  director-player. This is legitimate if that is still the audience (P-002).

### 5.3 A test that costs no code

Before any build, play three generic briefs in the current Free Game, reading
facets from the details card (the *i* control). They work with any draw:

1. **Ladder.** Pick two beads from different faculties that share no facet.
   Join them in at most three threads, each between beads that share a facet.
2. **Canon.** Pick a facet shown on three or more beads. Carry it through three
   faculties.
3. **Silence.** Find a facet that no bead of one faculty carries in this draw,
   and say so.

If these are not fun on paper, do not build them.

## 6. Implementation sketch — small parts, no rewrite

- `src/content/castalia/studies.ts` — authored `StudyDefinition`: id, chapter,
  bead set, brief, goal, budget, the Magister's solution. Validated like the
  rest of the pack.
- `src/domain/studies/evaluateStudy.ts` — pure `(state, study, lookup) →
  status`. It reuses `buildTopology`, `detectMotifs` and `sharedFacetsOf`, and
  imports no browser, React, Three.js or Web Audio code.
- `src/domain/studies/solve.ts` — a breadth-first solver. The validator uses it
  to prove each authored Study solvable, or each Silence Study impossible.
  Later it drives the Daily generator.
- **Identity without a schema change:** `session.started.seed` is
  `"study:<id>"` and `conceptIds` are the Study's beads. The goal is a pure
  lookup by id — the pattern ADR-013 already uses for relations. Restart is a
  new session with the same seed.
- **Presentation:** facet glyphs on the beads; the brief in the margin;
  *solved* as one cue through the cue boundary (ADR-009).
- **Tests:** each Study is solvable or impossible as declared; evaluation is
  stable under replay; no Study goal reads documented-relation presence (rule
  1 of §3.1).

## 8. Decisions for the director

- **D1.** Accept goals, completion and marks of form in Studies only — a scoped
  amendment to product law 7 and ADR-010.
- **D2.** Accept the two honesty rules in §3.1 as binding for every Study.
- **D3.** Retract: approve `thread.retracted` as the first schema-version-2
  change, or defer it (the spike uses restart).
- **D4.** First run: open on Eschholz Study 1 as the onboarding, or on the
  Free Game.
- **D5.** Names: *Studies* and *Free Game*, or another pair.

---

## 9. Risks

- **Glyph matching.** Players may match symbols and ignore meaning. Mitigation:
  each thread still opens its card, and Silence and Dialectic Studies need
  understanding, not matching.
- **Two half-modes.** Mitigation: Studies are a thin frame over the same loop.
  Build the spike, play it, then decide.
- **A content ceiling.** Twenty-four concepts support perhaps thirty to fifty
  good authored Studies. The Daily generator extends that, and each new concept
  adds many.
- **The contemplative identity thins out.** Mitigation: Free Game keeps
  everything it has now, and Studies have no timers and nothing to lose.

## Appendix — how the numbers were produced

- Pair counts, facet holders and ladder solutions: computed from
  `src/content/castalia/concepts.ts` and `relations.ts` at `171452d`. *Unlit*
  means no shared facet and no documented relation; *Open Thread material*
  means a shared facet and no documented relation.

  (≈ 29) strings across all 44 relations.
- Resonance bands: the accessible labels on the bead controls while Fibonacci
